Chapter 2.2: Disk and Washer Method

Introduction

When we revolve a region around an axis, we create a solid of revolution. The disk and washer methods are techniques for finding the volume of such solids.

The disk method applies when we revolve a single curve around an axis. When there is a hole in the center (from revolving a region between two curves), we use the washer method.

Theory

Disk Method

When a curve $y = f(x) \geq 0$ is revolved around the x-axis, the volume is:

$$V = \pi \int_a^b [f(x)]^2 \, dx$$

Each cross-section perpendicular to the axis of revolution is a disk with radius $R(x) = f(x)$ and area $\pi R^2$.

Washer Method

When we revolve a region between two curves around an axis, we get washers (disks with holes). If $f(x) \geq g(x) \geq 0$ and we revolve around the x-axis:

$$V = \pi \int_a^b \left([R(x)]^2 - [r(x)]^2\right) dx$$

where $R(x)$ is the outer radius and $r(x)$ is the inner radius.

Horizontal Slices

If revolving around the y-axis, integrate with respect to $y$:

$$V = \pi \int_c^d [f(y)]^2 \, dy$$

Method: Setting Up Disk and Washer Integrals

Steps for Disk/Washer Method:

  1. Identify the axis of revolution (x-axis, y-axis, or other)
  2. Determine the bounds of integration
  3. Find the outer radius $R(x)$ (or $R(y)$) — the distance from the axis to the outer curve
  4. If applicable, find the inner radius $r(x)$ (or $r(y)$) — the distance from the axis to the inner curve
  5. Apply the disk formula (if $r(x) = 0$) or washer formula (if $r(x) > 0$)
  6. Evaluate the integral

Important: When the axis of revolution is not a boundary of the region, account for the distance from the axis to the curve.

Examples

Example 1: Revolve $y = \sqrt{x}$ About the x-axis

Find the volume of the solid generated by revolving the region under $y = \sqrt{x}$ from $x = 0$ to $x = 4$ about the x-axis.

Setup:

The outer radius is $R(x) = \sqrt{x}$, and there is no hole, so we use the disk method.

$$V = \pi \int_0^4 (\sqrt{x})^2 \, dx = \pi \int_0^4 x \, dx$$

Evaluation:

$$V = \pi \left[\frac{x^2}{2}\right]_0^4 = \pi \cdot \frac{16}{2} = 8\pi$$

Example 2: Revolve Region Between $y = x^2$ and $y = x$ About the x-axis

Find the volume when the region between $y = x^2$ and $y = x$ (with $x^2 \leq x$ for $x \in [0,1]$) is revolved about the x-axis.

Setup:

The outer radius is $R(x) = x$ and the inner radius is $r(x) = x^2$.

$$V = \pi \int_0^1 \left[x^2 - (x^2)^2\right] dx = \pi \int_0^1 (x^2 - x^4) \, dx$$

Evaluation:

$$V = \pi \left[\frac{x^3}{3} - \frac{x^5}{5}\right]_0^1 = \pi \left(\frac{1}{3} - \frac{1}{5}\right) = \pi \cdot \frac{5-3}{15} = \frac{2\pi}{15}$$

Example 3: Revolve $y = x^2$ About the y-axis

Find the volume when $y = x^2$ from $x = 0$ to $x = 2$ is revolved about the y-axis.

Setup:

We express $x$ in terms of $y$: $x = \sqrt{y}$. When $x = 0$ to $x = 2$, we have $y = 0$ to $y = 4$.

The radius at height $y$ is $R(y) = \sqrt{y}$.

$$V = \pi \int_0^4 (\sqrt{y})^2 \, dy = \pi \int_0^4 y \, dy$$

Evaluation:

$$V = \pi \left[\frac{y^2}{2}\right]_0^4 = \pi \cdot \frac{16}{2} = 8\pi$$

Practice Problems

Problem 1

Find the volume of the solid obtained by revolving the region under $y = 2x - x^2$ from $x = 0$ to $x = 2$ about the x-axis.

Solution

$$V = \pi \int_0^2 (2x - x^2)^2 \, dx$$ $$= \pi \int_0^2 (4x^2 - 4x^3 + x^4) \, dx$$ $$= \pi \left[\frac{4x^3}{3} - x^4 + \frac{x^5}{5}\right]_0^2$$ $$= \pi \left(\frac{32}{3} - 16 + \frac{32}{5}\right)$$ $$= \pi \left(\frac{160 - 240 + 96}{15}\right) = \frac{16\pi}{15}$$

Problem 2

Find the volume of the solid obtained by revolving the region between $y = 2$ and $y = x$ from $x = 0$ to $x = 2$ about the x-axis.

Solution

The outer radius is $R(x) = 2$ and the inner radius is $r(x) = x$ (for $x \leq 2$).

$$V = \pi \int_0^2 (4 - x^2) \, dx$$ $$= \pi \left[4x - \frac{x^3}{3}\right]_0^2$$ $$= \pi \left(8 - \frac{8}{3}\right) = \frac{16\pi}{3}$$

Problem 3

Find the volume of the solid obtained by revolving the region under $y = e^{-x}$ from $x = 0$ to $x = 1$ about the x-axis.

Solution

$$V = \pi \int_0^1 (e^{-x})^2 \, dx = \pi \int_0^1 e^{-2x} \, dx$$ $$= \pi \left[-\frac{1}{2}e^{-2x}\right]_0^1$$ $$= \pi \left(-\frac{1}{2}e^{-2} + \frac{1}{2}\right)$$ $$= \frac{\pi}{2}(1 - e^{-2})$$
