Chapter 4.3: Integral & Comparison Tests

Learning Objectives
  • Apply the Integral Test to determine series convergence
  • Master p-series and their convergence criteria
  • Use the Direct Comparison Test effectively
  • Apply the Limit Comparison Test to compare series

Introduction

The Integral Test, Direct Comparison Test, and Limit Comparison Test are powerful tools for determining the convergence or divergence of series. These tests allow us to compare series behavior to integrals or to other known series.

Key topics:

  • Integral test and its geometric interpretation
  • p-series and their convergence criteria
  • Direct comparison test
  • Limit comparison test

Theory

Theorem: The Integral Test

Let $f(x)$ be a function that is positive, continuous, and decreasing on $[1, \infty)$, and let $a_n = f(n)$.

Then the series $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n$ and the integral $\int_1^{\infty} f(x) \, dx$ either both converge or both diverge.

The series converges if the integral converges, and diverges if the integral diverges.

Definition: p-Series

A p-series has the form:

$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^p}$$

Convergence criterion:

  • Converges if $p > 1$
  • Diverges if $p \le 1$

Proof: Apply the integral test to $f(x) = \frac{1}{x^p}$:

$$\int_1^{\infty} \frac{1}{x^p} \, dx = \lim_{t \to \infty} \int_1^{t} x^{-p} \, dx$$

For $p \neq 1$:

$$= \lim_{t \to \infty} \left[\frac{x^{1-p}}{1-p}\right]_1^{t} = \lim_{t \to \infty} \frac{1}{1-p}\left(t^{1-p} - 1\right)$$

This converges if $1 - p < 0$, i.e., $p > 1$.

Theorem: Direct Comparison Test

Suppose $0 \le a_n \le b_n$ for all $n$ beyond some point.

  • If $\sum b_n$ converges, then $\sum a_n$ converges
  • If $\sum a_n$ diverges, then $\sum b_n$ diverges
Theorem: Limit Comparison Test

Suppose $a_n > 0$ and $b_n > 0$, and suppose

$$\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{a_n}{b_n} = c$$

where $c > 0$ is finite. Then $\sum a_n$ and $\sum b_n$ either both converge or both diverge.

This test works best when $b_n$ is a simple series whose convergence is known.

💡 Pro Tip

For the Limit Comparison Test, identify the dominant term in your series. For example, in $\sum \frac{2n^2 + 3}{n^4 - n + 5}$, the dominant behavior is $\frac{2n^2}{n^4} = \frac{2}{n^2}$, so compare with $\sum \frac{1}{n^2}$.

Method: Choosing and Applying Tests

When to Use Each Test

Test Best For Key Condition
Integral Test Series from decreasing functions Easy integral to compute
p-Series $\sum 1/n^p$ Recognize form directly
Direct Comparison Clear term bounds $0 \le a_n \le b_n$
Limit Comparison Behavior similar to known series Limit exists and is positive

General Strategy

  1. Identify the form of $a_n$
  2. Determine dominant behavior (what matters as $n \to \infty$)
  3. Choose a comparison series with known convergence
  4. Apply appropriate test
  5. Conclude about original series
⚠️ Common Mistake

In the Direct Comparison Test, you need $0 \le a_n \le b_n$. If your series is larger than a divergent series, you can't conclude anything. Only use: (1) If $a_n \le b_n$ and $b_n$ converges, then $a_n$ converges, or (2) If $a_n \ge b_n$ and $b_n$ diverges, then $a_n$ diverges.

Examples

Example 1: p-Series $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^2}$

Determine if $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^2}$ converges.

Solution ▼

This is a p-series with $p = 2$.

Since $p = 2 > 1$, the series converges.

We can verify using the integral test:

$$\int_1^{\infty} \frac{1}{x^2} \, dx = \lim_{t \to \infty} \left[-\frac{1}{x}\right]_1^{t} = \lim_{t \to \infty} \left(1 - \frac{1}{t}\right) = 1$$

The integral converges, so the series converges.

Example 2: Direct Comparison $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^2 + 1}$

Determine if $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^2 + 1}$ converges.

Solution ▼

Notice that $n^2 + 1 > n^2$, so:

$$\frac{1}{n^2 + 1} < \frac{1}{n^2}$$

Since $\sum \frac{1}{n^2}$ converges (p-series with $p = 2 > 1$), by the Direct Comparison Test, $\sum \frac{1}{n^2 + 1}$ converges.

Example 3: Limit Comparison $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{\ln n}{n^2}$

Determine if $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{\ln n}{n^2}$ converges.

Solution ▼

Since $\frac{\ln n}{n^2} \le \frac{n}{n^2} = \frac{1}{n}$ is false for large $n$, and $\frac{\ln n}{n^2} \le \frac{1}{n^{3/2}}$ for large $n$ (which converges), $\sum \frac{\ln n}{n^2}$ converges by direct comparison with $\sum \frac{1}{n^{3/2}}$.

Alternatively, using Limit Comparison with $b_n = \frac{1}{n^2}$:

$$\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{\ln n / n^2}{1/n^2} = \lim_{n \to \infty} \ln n = \infty$$

The limit is infinite, so this comparison doesn't work directly. However, by the direct comparison argument above, the series converges.

Practice Problems

Practice Problem 1

Determine the convergence or divergence of $\sum_{n=2}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n \ln n}$. Hint: Use the integral test.

Show Solution ▼

Let $f(x) = \frac{1}{x \ln x}$. This is positive, continuous, and decreasing for $x \ge 2$.

Using the integral test:

$$\int_2^{\infty} \frac{1}{x \ln x} \, dx = \lim_{t \to \infty} \int_2^{t} \frac{1}{x \ln x} \, dx$$

Let $u = \ln x$, so $du = \frac{1}{x} dx$:

$$= \lim_{t \to \infty} \left[\ln(\ln x)\right]_2^{t} = \lim_{t \to \infty} (\ln(\ln t) - \ln(\ln 2)) = \infty$$

The integral diverges, so the series diverges.

Practice Problem 2

Use the direct comparison test to determine convergence of $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{3}{n^2 + 2n}$.

Show Solution ▼

For $n \ge 1$: $n^2 + 2n \ge n^2$, so:

$$\frac{3}{n^2 + 2n} \le \frac{3}{n^2}$$

Since $\sum \frac{3}{n^2} = 3 \sum \frac{1}{n^2}$ converges (p-series with $p = 2$), by the direct comparison test, $\sum \frac{3}{n^2 + 2n}$ converges.

Practice Problem 3

Use the limit comparison test to determine convergence of $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{2n + 3}{n^3 - n + 1}$.

Show Solution ▼

Compare with $b_n = \frac{1}{n^2}$ (a convergent p-series):

$$\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{\frac{2n+3}{n^3-n+1}}{\frac{1}{n^2}} = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{(2n+3)n^2}{n^3-n+1}$$

$$= \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{2n^3 + 3n^2}{n^3 - n + 1} = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{2 + \frac{3}{n}}{1 - \frac{1}{n^2} + \frac{1}{n^3}} = 2$$

Since the limit is a positive constant (2) and $\sum \frac{1}{n^2}$ converges, by the limit comparison test, $\sum \frac{2n+3}{n^3-n+1}$ converges.
