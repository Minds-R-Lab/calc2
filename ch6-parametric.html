Chapter 6.1: Parametric Curves

A parametric curve is a curve in the plane defined by two functions of a parameter $t$: $$x = f(t), \quad y = g(t), \quad t \in [\alpha, \beta]$$

Instead of expressing $y$ as a function of $x$, we express both $x$ and $y$ as functions of an independent parameter. This allows us to represent curves that fail the vertical line test and to naturally describe motion and time-dependent phenomena.

Theory

Derivatives of Parametric Curves

When $x$ and $y$ are given parametrically, we can find the slope of the tangent line using: $$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy/dt}{dx/dt} = \frac{g'(t)}{f'(t)}$$ provided $\frac{dx}{dt} \neq 0$.

The second derivative is: $$\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = \frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right) = \frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right) / \frac{dx}{dt}$$

Arc Length of Parametric Curves

The arc length of a parametric curve from $t = \alpha$ to $t = \beta$ is: $$L = \int_{\alpha}^{\beta} \sqrt{\left(\frac{dx}{dt}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{dy}{dt}\right)^2}\,dt$$

This formula is analogous to the arc length formula for Cartesian curves, but adapted to parametric form.

Tangent Lines

The equation of the tangent line to a parametric curve at $(x(t_0), y(t_0))$ is: $$y - y(t_0) = \frac{dy/dt|_{t_0}}{dx/dt|_{t_0}} (x - x(t_0))$$ provided $\frac{dx}{dt}\big|_{t_0} \neq 0$.

Method

For Finding $\frac{dy}{dx}$:

  1. Compute $\frac{dx}{dt}$ and $\frac{dy}{dt}$ separately.
  2. Divide: $\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy/dt}{dx/dt}$

For Finding Arc Length:

  1. Compute $\frac{dx}{dt}$ and $\frac{dy}{dt}$.
  2. Form the integrand: $\sqrt{\left(\frac{dx}{dt}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{dy}{dt}\right)^2}$
  3. Evaluate $\int_{\alpha}^{\beta} \sqrt{\left(\frac{dx}{dt}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{dy}{dt}\right)^2}\,dt$

For Finding Tangent Lines:

  1. Find the point $(x(t_0), y(t_0))$.
  2. Compute the slope $\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy/dt}{dx/dt}$ at $t = t_0$.
  3. Use point-slope form: $y - y(t_0) = m(x - x(t_0))$

Examples

Example 1: The Cycloid

Study the parametric curve $x = t - \sin t$, $y = 1 - \cos t$ for $t \in [0, 2\pi]$.

Solution:

This is a cycloid, the curve traced by a point on the rim of a circle of radius 1 rolling along the x-axis.

Derivatives: $$\frac{dx}{dt} = 1 - \cos t, \quad \frac{dy}{dt} = \sin t$$

The slope of the tangent line: $$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{\sin t}{1 - \cos t}$$

The arc length from $t = 0$ to $t = 2\pi$: $$L = \int_0^{2\pi} \sqrt{(1-\cos t)^2 + \sin^2 t}\,dt$$

Simplifying the integrand: $$(1-\cos t)^2 + \sin^2 t = 1 - 2\cos t + \cos^2 t + \sin^2 t = 2 - 2\cos t = 2(1 - \cos t)$$

So: $$L = \int_0^{2\pi} \sqrt{2(1-\cos t)}\,dt$$

Using the identity $1 - \cos t = 2\sin^2(t/2)$: $$L = \int_0^{2\pi} \sqrt{4\sin^2(t/2)}\,dt = \int_0^{2\pi} 2|\sin(t/2)|\,dt = 2\int_0^{2\pi} \sin(t/2)\,dt$$

(since $\sin(t/2) \geq 0$ for $t \in [0, 2\pi]$)

$$= 2 \left[-2\cos(t/2)\right]_0^{2\pi} = -4[\cos(\pi) - \cos(0)] = -4(-1 - 1) = 8$$

Example 2: Tangent to $x = t^2$, $y = t^3$ at $t = 1$

Find the equation of the tangent line at $t = 1$.

Solution:

Derivatives: $$\frac{dx}{dt} = 2t, \quad \frac{dy}{dt} = 3t^2$$

At $t = 1$: $$\frac{dx}{dt}\bigg|_{t=1} = 2, \quad \frac{dy}{dt}\bigg|_{t=1} = 3$$

Slope: $$\frac{dy}{dx}\bigg|_{t=1} = \frac{3}{2}$$

Point on curve at $t = 1$: $$x(1) = 1, \quad y(1) = 1$$

Tangent line equation: $$y - 1 = \frac{3}{2}(x - 1)$$ $$y = \frac{3}{2}x - \frac{1}{2}$$

Example 3: Arc Length of the Unit Circle

Find the arc length of $x = \cos t$, $y = \sin t$ from $t = 0$ to $t = 2\pi$.

Solution:

Derivatives: $$\frac{dx}{dt} = -\sin t, \quad \frac{dy}{dt} = \cos t$$

Arc length: $$L = \int_0^{2\pi} \sqrt{\sin^2 t + \cos^2 t}\,dt = \int_0^{2\pi} 1\,dt = 2\pi$$

This confirms that the circumference of a unit circle is $2\pi$.

Practice Problems

Problem 1

For the parametric curve $x = e^t \cos t$, $y = e^t \sin t$, find $\frac{dy}{dx}$ at $t = 0$.

Solution

Using the product rule: $$\frac{dx}{dt} = e^t \cos t - e^t \sin t = e^t(\cos t - \sin t)$$ $$\frac{dy}{dt} = e^t \sin t + e^t \cos t = e^t(\sin t + \cos t)$$

At $t = 0$: $$\frac{dx}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0} = e^0(1 - 0) = 1$$ $$\frac{dy}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0} = e^0(0 + 1) = 1$$

Therefore: $$\frac{dy}{dx}\bigg|_{t=0} = \frac{1}{1} = 1$$

Problem 2

Find the arc length of $x = \frac{t^2}{2}$, $y = \frac{t^3}{3}$ from $t = 0$ to $t = 2$.

Solution

Derivatives: $$\frac{dx}{dt} = t, \quad \frac{dy}{dt} = t^2$$

Arc length: $$L = \int_0^2 \sqrt{t^2 + t^4}\,dt = \int_0^2 \sqrt{t^2(1 + t^2)}\,dt = \int_0^2 t\sqrt{1 + t^2}\,dt$$

Let $u = 1 + t^2$, so $du = 2t\,dt$: $$L = \frac{1}{2}\int_1^5 \sqrt{u}\,du = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{2}{3}u^{3/2}\bigg|_1^5 = \frac{1}{3}(5^{3/2} - 1)$$

$$= \frac{1}{3}(5\sqrt{5} - 1) \approx \frac{1}{3}(11.18 - 1) = \frac{10.18}{3} \approx 3.39$$

Problem 3

For $x = 3\cos t$, $y = 4\sin t$ (an ellipse), find the point(s) where the tangent line is horizontal.

Solution

Derivatives: $$\frac{dx}{dt} = -3\sin t, \quad \frac{dy}{dt} = 4\cos t$$

The tangent line is horizontal when $\frac{dy}{dx} = 0$, which occurs when $\frac{dy}{dt} = 0$ and $\frac{dx}{dt} \neq 0$.

$\frac{dy}{dt} = 0$ when $4\cos t = 0$, i.e., $\cos t = 0$.

This happens at $t = \frac{\pi}{2}$ and $t = \frac{3\pi}{2}$.

At $t = \frac{\pi}{2}$: $x = 0$, $y = 4$ $\to$ point $(0, 4)$

At $t = \frac{3\pi}{2}$: $x = 0$, $y = -4$ $\to$ point $(0, -4)$

Tangent lines are horizontal at $(0, 4)$ and $(0, -4)$.
