Chapter 5.1: Power Series

A power series is a function series of the form $$\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} c_n(x-a)^n = c_0 + c_1(x-a) + c_2(x-a)^2 + c_3(x-a)^3 + \cdots$$ where $c_n$ are constant coefficients and $a$ is the center of expansion.

Power series are fundamental in calculus because they allow us to represent functions as infinite polynomials. Unlike other series, the convergence of a power series depends on the value of $x$. Understanding where a power series converges—its radius and interval of convergence—is essential.

Theory

Radius and Interval of Convergence

Every power series $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} c_n(x-a)^n$ has a radius of convergence $R \geq 0$ such that:

  • The series converges absolutely for all $x$ with $|x-a| < R$
  • The series diverges for all $x$ with $|x-a| > R$
  • At the endpoints $x = a \pm R$, the series may converge or diverge (must check)

The interval of convergence is the set of all $x$ values for which the series converges. It always has the form $[a-R, a+R]$, $(a-R, a+R)$, $[a-R, a+R)$, or $(a-R, a+R]$, depending on whether the endpoints converge.

Finding the Radius of Convergence: Ratio Test

The most common method uses the ratio test: $$\lim_{n \to \infty} \left| \frac{c_{n+1}}{c_n} \right| = L$$

Then the radius of convergence is: $$R = \frac{1}{L}$$ (with conventions: if $L = 0$, then $R = \infty$; if $L = \infty$, then $R = 0$).

Checking Endpoints

After finding $R$, substitute $x = a + R$ and $x = a - R$ into the series and test for convergence using standard convergence tests (alternating series test, p-test, etc.).

Method: Finding Radius and Interval of Convergence

  1. Apply the Ratio Test: Calculate $\lim_{n \to \infty} \left|\frac{c_{n+1}(x-a)^{n+1}}{c_n(x-a)^n}\right|$ Simplify to get $|x-a| \cdot L$, where $L = \lim_{n \to \infty} \left|\frac{c_{n+1}}{c_n}\right|$.
  2. Set up inequality: For convergence, we need $|x-a| \cdot L < 1$, which gives $|x-a| < \frac{1}{L}$. Thus $R = \frac{1}{L}$.
  3. Check endpoint $x = a + R$: Substitute and test the resulting series.
  4. Check endpoint $x = a - R$: Substitute and test the resulting series.
  5. Write the interval of convergence: Include endpoints if they converge; exclude if they diverge.

Examples

Example 1: $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{n}$

Find the radius and interval of convergence.

Solution:

Here $a = 0$, $c_n = \frac{1}{n}$.

Apply the ratio test: $$\lim_{n \to \infty} \left|\frac{c_{n+1}}{c_n}\right| = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{1/(n+1)}{1/n} = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{n}{n+1} = 1$$

Therefore $R = \frac{1}{1} = 1$, and we check $x = \pm 1$.

At $x = 1$: $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n}$ is the harmonic series, which diverges.

At $x = -1$: $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{n}$ is the alternating harmonic series, which converges.

Interval of convergence: $[-1, 1)$

Example 2: $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} n! \, x^n$

Find the radius and interval of convergence.

Solution:

Here $c_n = n!$.

Apply the ratio test: $$\lim_{n \to \infty} \left|\frac{(n+1)!}{n!}\right| = \lim_{n \to \infty} (n+1) = \infty$$

Therefore $R = \frac{1}{\infty} = 0$. The series converges only at $x = 0$.

Example 3: $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{n!}$

Find the radius and interval of convergence.

Solution:

Here $c_n = \frac{1}{n!}$.

Apply the ratio test: $$\lim_{n \to \infty} \left|\frac{1/(n+1)!}{1/n!}\right| = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{n!}{(n+1)!} = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{1}{n+1} = 0$$

Therefore $R = \frac{1}{0} = \infty$. The series converges for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$.

Interval of convergence: $(-\infty, \infty)$

Practice Problems

Problem 1

Find the radius and interval of convergence for $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(x-2)^n}{n \cdot 2^n}$.

Solution

Here $a = 2$ and $c_n = \frac{1}{n \cdot 2^n}$.

Apply the ratio test: $$\lim_{n \to \infty} \left|\frac{c_{n+1}}{c_n}\right| = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{n \cdot 2^n}{(n+1) \cdot 2^{n+1}} = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{n}{2(n+1)} = \frac{1}{2}$$

Therefore $R = \frac{1}{1/2} = 2$.

Check $x = 2 + 2 = 4$: $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{2^n}{n \cdot 2^n} = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n}$ diverges.

Check $x = 2 - 2 = 0$: $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-2)^n}{n \cdot 2^n} = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{n}$ converges.

Interval of convergence: $[0, 4)$

Problem 2

Find the radius of convergence for $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{n^2 x^n}{5^n}$.

Solution

Here $c_n = \frac{n^2}{5^n}$.

Apply the ratio test: $$\lim_{n \to \infty} \left|\frac{(n+1)^2 / 5^{n+1}}{n^2 / 5^n}\right| = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{(n+1)^2}{5n^2} = \frac{1}{5} \lim_{n \to \infty} \left(\frac{n+1}{n}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{5}$$

Therefore $R = \frac{1}{1/5} = 5$.

Radius of convergence: $R = 5$

Problem 3

Determine the interval of convergence for $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n x^n}{n^2}$.

Solution

Here $c_n = \frac{(-1)^n}{n^2}$.

Apply the ratio test (ignoring the $(-1)^n$ factor): $$\lim_{n \to \infty} \left|\frac{1/(n+1)^2}{1/n^2}\right| = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{n^2}{(n+1)^2} = 1$$

Therefore $R = 1$.

Check $x = 1$: $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{n^2}$ converges absolutely (p-series with $p = 2 > 1$).

Check $x = -1$: $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n(-1)^n}{n^2} = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^2}$ converges absolutely.

Interval of convergence: $[-1, 1]$
