1.5 Which Technique Should I Use?

Learning Objectives
  • Recognize visual patterns in integrands that point to specific techniques
  • Use a systematic decision process to select the right integration method
  • Understand when multiple techniques might work and how to choose the most efficient one

One of the hardest parts of Chapter 1 isn't learning the individual techniques — it's knowing which one to use when you face a new integral. This page gives you two tools: a visual flowchart you can follow step by step, and an interactive wizard that asks you questions about your integrand and tells you what to try.

Golden Rule

Always try simplification and u-substitution first. They're the fastest methods. Only move to the advanced techniques (by parts, trig sub, partial fractions) when simpler approaches don't work.

The Decision Flowchart

Start at the top and follow the arrows based on what your integrand looks like. Each path leads to the recommended technique.

Common Mistake

Students often jump to an advanced technique without first checking if basic algebra or u-substitution would work. For example, $\int \frac{2x}{x^2+1}dx$ doesn't need partial fractions — it's a simple u-sub with $u = x^2+1$!

Pro Tip: The "Try and Fail" Strategy

If you're stuck, just start trying a technique. If it makes the integral simpler, you're on the right track. If it makes it more complicated, stop and try a different approach. Experienced mathematicians do this all the time — don't be afraid to backtrack.

Practice: Name That Technique

For each integral below, identify the technique you'd use before revealing the answer.

1. $\int x e^{3x}\,dx$
Show Answer ▼

Integration by Parts — Product of polynomial ($x$) and exponential ($e^{3x}$). Let $u = x$, $dv = e^{3x}dx$.

2. $\int \frac{5x+1}{x^2+x-6}\,dx$
Show Answer ▼

Partial Fractions — Rational function with factorable denominator: $x^2+x-6 = (x+3)(x-2)$. Decompose into $\frac{A}{x+3} + \frac{B}{x-2}$.

3. $\int \sin^4 x\cos^3 x\,dx$
Show Answer ▼

Trig Integrals — Powers of sin and cos. Since cos has an odd power, save one $\cos x$ for $du$, convert $\cos^2 x = 1 - \sin^2 x$, and substitute $u = \sin x$.

4. $\int \frac{x^2}{\sqrt{9-x^2}}\,dx$
Show Answer ▼

Trig Substitution — Contains $\sqrt{a^2 - x^2}$ with $a = 3$. Use $x = 3\sin\theta$.

5. $\int \frac{2x}{x^2+4}\,dx$
Show Answer ▼

U-Substitution — The numerator $2x$ is the derivative of the denominator $x^2+4$. Let $u = x^2+4$, $du = 2x\,dx$. Result: $\ln|x^2+4| + C$.

6. $\int e^x \sin x\,dx$
Show Answer ▼

Integration by Parts (twice) — Product of exponential and trig. Apply by parts twice, then the original integral reappears — solve the resulting equation for $I$.

7. $\int \frac{x^3+2}{x^2-1}\,dx$
Show Answer ▼

Long division first, then Partial Fractions. Since $\deg(\text{top}) > \deg(\text{bottom})$, divide: $\frac{x^3+2}{x^2-1} = x + \frac{x+2}{x^2-1}$. Then decompose the remainder using partial fractions.
